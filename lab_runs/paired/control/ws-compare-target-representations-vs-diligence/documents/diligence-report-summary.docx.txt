I. Executive Summary
Cascadia Industrial Holdings, Inc. (NYSE: CIHI) ("Buyer" or "Cascadia") proposes to acquire one hundred percent (100%) of the outstanding common stock of PrecisionFlow Systems, Inc., a Delaware corporation headquartered in Dayton, Ohio ("PrecisionFlow," the "Target," or the "Company"), for an aggregate purchase price of $287 million, representing a 9.5x multiple of adjusted EBITDA of $30.21 million. The transaction is structured as a stock purchase and is governed by a draft Stock Purchase Agreement (the "SPA") circulated by Stonebridge Whitaker LLP (Jeffrey Denton, lead partner), counsel to the Sellers, on February 28, 2025.
PrecisionFlow is a manufacturer of hydraulic control systems, including proportional valves, directional control valves, and integrated fluid power assemblies. The Company was founded in 2004 by Harold "Hal" Beecham and maintains manufacturing operations in Dayton, Ohio and Monterrey, Mexico (through its wholly-owned subsidiary, PrecisionFlow de México, S.A. de C.V.). For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company reported revenue of $143.0 million and employs approximately 740 people across both facilities.
The parties intend to execute the SPA on March 14, 2025, with a target closing date of June 30, 2025. An escrow deposit of $22.96 million (8% of the aggregate purchase price) will be held for eighteen (18) months following the closing to secure the Sellers' indemnification obligations.
Overall Assessment. PrecisionFlow is a fundamentally sound business with attractive financial characteristics, including strong revenue growth (CAGR of approximately 10% from FY2022 through FY2024), solid gross margins (34.2% in FY2024), and a product portfolio that is complementary to Cascadia's existing Fluid Power division. Notwithstanding the foregoing, our diligence has identified a number of material items requiring attention before signing, which are described in detail in the body of this report and summarized below:
•  Intellectual Property: Two patents directly related to the PF-4200 proportional valve are omitted from Disclosure Schedule 3.9, and the non-infringement opinion obtained in connection with the AquaDyne Technologies cease-and-desist letter is materially incomplete due to Target's refusal to produce internal testing data.
•  Material Contracts: The Kessler Heavy Industries MSA contains an undisclosed change-of-control termination right affecting 20.6% of revenue; the NorthPoint Fabrication MSA ($18.6M in annual revenue) expired on February 28, 2025, and has not been renewed.
•  Environmental: The Phase I Environmental Site Assessment identified a recognized environmental condition at the Dayton facility that is not disclosed in Schedule 3.12, and no Phase II investigation has been completed.
•  Tax: Target has California income tax nexus exposure arising from an unreported physical presence and approximately $3.8 million in California-sourced sales, with no California returns filed since at least 2022.
•  Financial: A $0.60 million EBITDA add-back characterized as COVID-related supply chain costs appears recurring in nature, and the pension plan unfunded liability is understated by approximately $2.5 million based on updated actuarial assumptions.
•  Litigation/Insurance: The Martinez product liability claim ($12M demand) exceeds the per-occurrence insurance limit ($10M) by $2 million, creating a potential coverage gap.
•  Corporate: Hal Beecham's 2021 divorce settlement contains a disputed provision potentially encumbering 3.1% of the outstanding shares.
•  Employment: An October 2024 OSHA citation is actively contested, rendering the SPA compliance representation technically inaccurate; the employee headcount in Schedule 3.17 is understated by 10.
•  Real Property: The Monterrey facility lease requires landlord consent for an indirect change of control, but the consent is not listed on the Required Consents schedule (Schedule 5.3).
Recommendation. We recommend that the deal team address the enumerated issues prior to signing through disclosure schedule corrections, additional targeted diligence, SPA modifications, and commercial actions as detailed in Section XIII of this report.

Tax Diligence. Tax diligence included a review of federal and state income tax returns for FY2019 through FY2024, R&D credit documentation and associated IRS correspondence, and Mexican subsidiary (PrecisionFlow de México) tax filings and transfer pricing documentation.
Actuarial Review. Buyer's independent actuary was retained by Ridgeline Capital Partners to evaluate the funded status of the Target's defined benefit pension plan using current market assumptions.

III.A. Shareholder Title Issues — Karen Beecham Disputed Claim
During our verification of the capitalization table and chain of title for all outstanding shares, we identified a potentially material encumbrance on a portion of Hal Beecham's equity interest.
Specifically, our review of public court records from the Montgomery County, Ohio Court of Common Pleas, Domestic Relations Division, reveals that Hal Beecham's divorce from his ex-wife, Karen Beecham, was finalized in 2021. The divorce settlement agreement, which was incorporated into the final decree, contains a provision awarding Karen Beecham a claim to five percent (5%) of Hal Beecham's shares of common stock in PrecisionFlow Systems, Inc. If valid and enforceable, this claim would encumber 5% of Hal's 62% interest, or approximately 3.1% of the total outstanding shares of the Company.
At the proposed aggregate purchase price of $287 million, 3.1% of equity value equates to approximately $8.9 million in share value that may be subject to Karen Beecham's claim.
Seller's counsel at Stonebridge Whitaker LLP (Jeffrey Denton) has represented informally during a telephone call on February 20, 2025 that Hal Beecham disputes this provision and that the matter is the subject of ongoing post-decree proceedings in the domestic relations court. However, no court order resolving the dispute has been produced in the data room, and no formal legal opinion has been provided regarding the validity or enforceability of Karen Beecham's claim. We note separately that Hal Beecham holds a $1.8 million personal guaranty on the Target's ABL credit facility with First Midwestern Bank, N.A., which will need to be released at closing in accordance with the terms of the SPA.
Risk Flag. If Karen Beecham's claim is valid and enforceable, Hal Beecham may not have clear title to the full 62% of shares attributed to him on the capitalization table. The SPA contains a representation by each Seller (Section 3.3) that such Seller holds "good and marketable title to all shares of Company Common Stock set forth opposite such Seller's name on the capitalization table, free and clear of all liens, encumbrances, pledges, security interests, claims, options, and restrictions of any kind." The potential Karen Beecham claim could constitute a breach of this representation and could delay or block the closing if not resolved.
We recommend the following actions: (1) obtain a certified court order resolving the post-decree dispute, or a signed release and waiver from Karen Beecham, prior to closing; (2) in the alternative, structure a portion of the $22.96 million escrow to specifically address the disputed 3.1% of shares (approximately $8.9 million) until the claim is resolved; and (3) require Seller's counsel to provide a formal legal opinion regarding the status of the dispute and the enforceability of the settlement provision.

IV.B. EBITDA Adjustments and Quality of Earnings Analysis
The Seller proposes total EBITDA adjustments of $3.80 million, yielding an adjusted EBITDA of $30.21 million ($26.41M reported + $3.80M adjustments). At the agreed 9.5x multiple, this produces the $287.0 million aggregate purchase price. The adjusted EBITDA margin is 21.13% ($30.21M ÷ $143.0M). The individual adjustments and our findings are as follows:
1. Hal Beecham Above-Market Compensation — $1.70M. This adjustment represents the difference between Beecham's total compensation (including above-market perquisites) and the estimated market-rate cost for a replacement CEO of comparable experience in a manufacturing company of this size and complexity. We reviewed comparable CEO compensation data from industry surveys and public company proxy disclosures. The adjustment appears supportable. No issue flagged.
2. One-Time ERP Implementation Costs — $0.85M. This adjustment relates to the implementation of a new enterprise resource planning system completed in Q2 2024. The costs are supported by invoices from the ERP vendor, and the implementation is confirmed as complete. The non-recurring nature of this charge is well-documented. No issue flagged.
3. Non-Recurring Legal Settlement Costs — $0.65M. This adjustment relates to the settlement of a commercial dispute with a former distributor in Q1 2024. The one-time nature of the charge is confirmed by the settlement agreement, which contains a full and final release. No issue flagged.
4. COVID-Related Supply Chain Disruption Charges — $0.60M. The Seller characterizes this adjustment as non-recurring costs attributable to COVID-related supply chain disruptions carried into early 2024, including expedited freight charges, alternative sourcing premiums, and inventory write-downs.
Risk Flag. Ridgeline Capital Partners' analysis of the $0.60 million COVID supply chain add-back has identified a significant concern regarding the characterization of these charges as non-recurring. Our review of the Company's financial records reveals that PrecisionFlow has incurred materially similar "supply chain disruption" charges in each of the prior two fiscal years: $0.55 million in FY2022 and $0.45 million in FY2023. The charges in all three years relate to the same categories of expense — expedited freight, alternative sourcing premiums, and inventory write-downs attributable to supply chain disruptions — and are booked to the same general ledger accounts.
While the Seller characterizes the FY2024 charges as COVID-related and therefore one-time in nature, the consistent appearance of materially similar charges across three consecutive fiscal years strongly suggests that these represent recurring operational costs embedded in the Company's supply chain cost structure rather than non-recurring items. We note further that the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services ended the COVID-19 public health emergency on May 11, 2023, making the characterization of charges incurred in FY2024 as "COVID-related" questionable at best.
If the $0.60 million add-back is rejected, adjusted EBITDA decreases from $30.21 million to $29.61 million. At the agreed 9.5x multiple, the implied enterprise value decreases by $5.7 million (from $287.0 million to $281.3 million). We recommend challenging this adjustment during purchase price negotiations.

IV.D. Pension and Retirement Plans — Liability Assessment
The Target maintains a 401(k) defined contribution plan with a 4% employer match. Our review of the plan documents, Form 5500 filings, and plan administration records indicates that the 401(k) plan appears compliant with applicable ERISA requirements. No issues were identified with respect to this plan.
The Target also maintains a defined benefit pension plan that was frozen as of December 31, 2018. No further benefit accruals have occurred since the freeze date. The most recent actuarial valuation provided by the Seller is dated January 2024 and reports an unfunded liability of $4.3 million (projected benefit obligation of approximately $18.9 million less plan assets of approximately $14.6 million).
However, Buyer's independent actuary, retained by Ridgeline Capital Partners, has performed an updated estimate of the plan's funded status incorporating current market conditions. The independent actuary's analysis specifically accounts for the material decline in interest rates during the second half of 2024, which resulted in a decrease in the applicable discount rate from approximately 5.4% (as used in the January 2024 valuation) to approximately 4.6% as of year-end 2024. The lower discount rate increases the present value of the plan's projected benefit obligation. The independent actuary's updated estimate places the unfunded liability at approximately $6.8 million — a $2.5 million increase over the Seller-provided valuation.
The SPA's benefits representation at Section 3.16 states that all employee benefit plans are "funded in accordance with all applicable requirements of ERISA and the Code" and specifically references the $4.3 million unfunded liability figure from the January 2024 valuation. The $2.5 million gap between the Seller's disclosed figure and the current estimate is material and suggests that the benefits representation may understate the actual pension obligation as of the anticipated closing date.
Risk Flag. The $2.5 million incremental pension liability should be addressed through either a purchase price adjustment, a specific indemnification obligation, or an updated actuarial valuation to be completed prior to closing. At a minimum, the SPA should be revised to reference a bring-down actuarial valuation to be performed within sixty (60) days of the closing date, with a true-up mechanism for any variance from the disclosed funded status.

V.A. Patent Portfolio
Our review of the United States Patent and Trademark Office ("USPTO") database and the Target's internal IP records confirms that PrecisionFlow holds fourteen (14) issued U.S. patents covering hydraulic valve designs and flow control algorithms, plus three (3) pending patent applications. We have verified all fourteen issued patents by number, issue date, and expiration date. The complete list of patents, including registration details, is set forth in the IP Diligence Memorandum prepared by the Holloway & Fitch IP group, which is incorporated herein by reference.
Of particular note, U.S. Patent No. 10,891,234 ("Proportional Valve Assembly with Integrated Flow Sensing") and U.S. Patent No. 10,891,235 ("Adaptive Control Algorithm for Proportional Hydraulic Valves") both relate specifically to the PF-4200 proportional valve product line, which is the product at the center of the AquaDyne Technologies infringement dispute discussed in Section V.C below.
Risk Flag. Seller's Disclosure Schedule 3.9 (Intellectual Property) lists only twelve (12) issued U.S. patents. The two omitted patents — U.S. Patent Nos. 10,891,234 and 10,891,235 — are the very patents that cover the PF-4200 proportional valve. This omission is of significant concern for several reasons: first, the omitted patents are directly relevant to the AquaDyne infringement dispute (Section V.C); second, the SPA's IP representation at Section 3.9 states that Schedule 3.9 sets forth a "true, correct, and complete" list of all Company Intellectual Property, which is inaccurate as drafted; and third, the omission — when viewed alongside Target's refusal to produce the PF-4200 internal testing data — raises questions about whether the incomplete disclosures are inadvertent or deliberate. Schedule 3.9 must be corrected prior to signing to include all fourteen issued patents and all three pending applications.

V.C. AquaDyne Infringement Dispute
On September 8, 2024, PrecisionFlow received a cease-and-desist letter from AquaDyne Technologies, LLC, a Delaware limited liability company, alleging that PrecisionFlow's Model PF-4200 proportional valve infringes AquaDyne's U.S. Patent No. 11,234,567 (issued January 2023). The AquaDyne patent is directed to proportional flow control valve assemblies and methods of proportional flow control.
In response, PrecisionFlow retained outside IP counsel at Hargrove & Lind LLP (Washington, D.C.) to evaluate the infringement claim. Hargrove & Lind issued a non-infringement opinion dated November 15, 2024, concluding that the PF-4200 proportional valve does not infringe the asserted claims of the AquaDyne patent.
Critical Finding. Our review of the Hargrove & Lind opinion reveals that it contains a significant and material caveat. Specifically, Hargrove & Lind states that their claim-by-claim analysis could not address claim 14 of U.S. Patent No. 11,234,567 because PrecisionFlow declined to produce certain internal testing data related to the PF-4200 valve's flow response characteristics. Claim 14 is a broad method claim directed to a proportional flow control methodology that, based on our review of the patent claims, appears to encompass the general operating principles described in PrecisionFlow's publicly available technical literature for the PF-4200.
The Hargrove & Lind opinion expressly states: "Our conclusions are limited to claims 1–13 and 15–22 of the '567 patent. We express no opinion as to claim 14 due to the unavailability of the testing data described herein."
No litigation has been filed by AquaDyne to date, but the cease-and-desist letter expressly reserves all rights, including the right to seek injunctive relief and damages.
Risk Flag. The non-infringement opinion is materially incomplete. The unanalyzed claim (claim 14) could form the basis for an infringement finding, and the breadth of the claim language suggests it presents the highest risk among all twenty-two claims of the AquaDyne patent. The Target's refusal to produce its own internal testing data to its own retained counsel raises serious questions about what that data would reveal regarding the PF-4200's operation relative to the method recited in claim 14. When combined with the omission of the two PF-4200-related patents from Schedule 3.9 (see Section V.A above), the AquaDyne dispute represents a significant and concentrated IP risk area. The SPA's IP representation regarding the existence and adequacy of non-infringement opinions may be misleadingly incomplete if the Hargrove & Lind caveat is not specifically disclosed.
Based on management estimates provided during presentations, PF-4200 product line revenue is approximately $22 million per year, representing approximately 15% of total FY2024 revenue. An adverse infringement finding — particularly one resulting in an injunction — could have a material impact on the Company's revenue and operations.
VI. Environmental
VI.A. Dayton Facility — 4500 Industrial Parkway, Dayton, OH 45414
The Dayton facility is a 12-acre owned site housing approximately 185,000 square feet of manufacturing and corporate office space. Our environmental diligence for this facility focused on a review of the Phase I Environmental Site Assessment conducted by Greenleaf Environmental Consulting, LLC (Dayton, OH), dated October 2024, and Ohio EPA correspondence.
Phase I ESA Findings. The Phase I ESA identified a recognized environmental condition ("REC") on the property. Specifically, the ESA documented historical solvent use by a prior tenant that occupied the property before PrecisionFlow acquired it in 2004. The REC relates to possible soil contamination in the northeast corner of the property associated with the prior tenant's operations, which are reported to have included degreasing activities using chlorinated solvents. Greenleaf Environmental Consulting's report expressly recommends that a Phase II subsurface investigation be conducted to delineate the nature and extent of potential contamination.
A Phase II investigation has not been completed. During management presentations, Target representatives stated that the Company does not believe the historical contamination poses a current risk to operations or to the surrounding community. However, no technical basis for this position was provided, and no sampling data, remediation records, or environmental consultant opinions supporting this conclusion were produced in the data room.
Separately, a 2023 hydraulic fluid spill (approximately 200 gallons, occurring in July 2023) was self-reported to the Ohio Environmental Protection Agency, remediated by Target, and a closure letter was received from Ohio EPA in September 2023. This matter appears to be fully resolved and does not present an ongoing concern.
Risk Flag. Seller's Disclosure Schedule 3.12 (Environmental) references only the 2023 hydraulic fluid spill and the corresponding Ohio EPA closure letter. The Phase I REC finding — historical solvent contamination in the northeast corner of the property — is not disclosed in Schedule 3.12. This omission renders the environmental representation at Section 3.12 of the SPA potentially inaccurate and incomplete. Without a Phase II investigation, the extent of contamination and the potential cost of remediation are unknown. Historical chlorinated solvent contamination is a well-recognized source of significant environmental liability under the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act ("CERCLA") and Ohio Revised Code Chapter 3746, and such contamination frequently involves groundwater migration extending beyond the originally affected area.
We recommend the following: (1) require the Seller to complete a Phase II subsurface investigation prior to closing, or establish a dedicated environmental escrow sufficient to cover potential remediation costs; (2) require correction of Schedule 3.12 to disclose the REC identified in the Phase I ESA; and (3) consider requiring an environmental indemnity or the procurement of a pollution legal liability insurance policy to address potential post-closing environmental liabilities.

Total OSHA penalties assessed across all three citations: $46,400.
Risk Flag. The October 2024 OSHA citation ($28,700) is actively contested and has not been resolved. The SPA representation on compliance with law (Section 3.11) states that the Company is "in material compliance with all applicable laws, statutes, regulations, and ordinances" and that all governmental orders, penalties, and citations have been "fully resolved." The pending OSHRC contest renders this representation technically inaccurate as of the signing date. While the $28,700 monetary penalty is not individually material in the context of a $287 million transaction, the characterization of all citations as "fully resolved" is factually incorrect. Moreover, the underlying lockout/tagout violation — which relates to one of the most common causes of serious workplace injuries in manufacturing environments — could indicate systemic safety concerns that warrant further investigation.
We recommend: (1) correction of the applicable disclosure schedule to identify the pending contest and its current status; (2) qualification of the Section 3.11 representation to expressly carve out the October 2024 citation; and (3) a review of the Company's lockout/tagout compliance program, including training records, written procedures, and periodic inspection documentation, to assess whether the cited violation is an isolated incident or indicative of a broader compliance deficiency.

VIII.B. Kessler Heavy Industries MSA
The Kessler Heavy Industries Master Supply Agreement ("Kessler MSA") is dated January 15, 2021. The agreement has an initial five-year term expiring January 14, 2026, with automatic two-year renewal periods unless either party provides 180 days' written notice of non-renewal.
Critical Finding. Section 12.3 of the Kessler MSA contains a change-of-control clause permitting Kessler to terminate the MSA on sixty (60) days' written notice following a "change in control" of PrecisionFlow. The term "change in control" is defined broadly in the Kessler MSA to include any transaction resulting in a change in the majority ownership or voting control of PrecisionFlow. The proposed acquisition by Cascadia — a purchase of 100% of the outstanding common stock — unambiguously constitutes a "change in control" under this definition.
Risk Flag. Disclosure Schedule 3.15 (Material Contracts) lists the Kessler MSA but does not disclose the existence of the change-of-control termination right contained in Section 12.3. The SPA requires disclosure of any contract containing change-of-control provisions, consent requirements, or termination triggers related to the contemplated transaction (Section 3.15(b)). Kessler is the Company's largest customer, representing $29.4 million — or 20.6% — of FY2024 revenue. If Kessler were to exercise its termination right post-closing, the resulting revenue loss would be highly material to the Company's financial performance and to the valuation assumptions underlying the purchase price.
We recommend: (1) immediate correction of Schedule 3.15 to disclose the Section 12.3 change-of-control termination right; (2) that Buyer consider requiring the Seller to obtain a written waiver or consent from Kessler Heavy Industries prior to closing, structured as a closing condition in the SPA; and (3) an assessment of whether additional purchase price protection mechanisms, such as a customer retention escrow or an earn-out component tied to Kessler revenue retention, are warranted.

IX. Litigation
IX.A. Martinez Product Liability Lawsuit
Martinez v. PrecisionFlow Systems, Inc., Case No. 2:23-cv-04178, United States District Court for the Southern District of Ohio, filed November 2023.
The plaintiff, a maintenance technician at a manufacturing facility operated by a PrecisionFlow customer, alleges that a PF-3100 hydraulic valve manufactured by the Company failed during normal operation, causing a hydraulic press malfunction that resulted in serious personal injuries to the plaintiff, including a crush injury to the right hand requiring multiple surgical procedures. The plaintiff seeks $12 million in compensatory and punitive damages.
Trial is currently scheduled for September 2025, which would occur post-closing. Discovery is ongoing.
The Company's liability insurer, Midland Casualty Insurance Co., has accepted the defense under a reservation of rights. The applicable commercial general liability policy provides coverage of $10 million per occurrence and $20 million in the aggregate.
Risk Flag. If the plaintiff were to obtain a judgment at or near the $12 million demand figure, there would be a $2 million coverage gap ($12 million demand minus the $10 million per-occurrence policy limit). The SPA's litigation representation (Section 3.10) states that all pending litigation is "adequately covered by insurance." Given the $2 million potential shortfall between the demand and the applicable policy limit, this representation may be inaccurate. While the ultimate outcome of the litigation remains uncertain, the possibility of a judgment exceeding policy limits is not a remote scenario given the severity of the plaintiff's alleged injuries and the nature of the claims.
Sandra Cho (General Counsel) characterized the claim as "defensible" during the February 5, 2025 management presentation. However, no written assessment from defense counsel regarding the merits, likely outcome, or recommended settlement authority was produced in the data room. We note further that the reservation of rights letter from Midland Casualty reserves the right to deny coverage for punitive damages if awarded under Ohio law.
We recommend: (1) obtaining defense counsel's written case assessment and settlement authority recommendation; (2) requesting modification of the SPA litigation representation to acknowledge the potential coverage gap and carve out the Martinez matter from the "adequately covered" language; and (3) evaluating whether a litigation-specific indemnity or escrow is warranted to protect against the uninsured exposure.
IX.B. Thompson EEOC Charge
Thompson v. PrecisionFlow Systems, Inc., EEOC Charge No. 473-2024-01892, filed June 2024. The charging party, a former plant supervisor at the Dayton facility, alleges race discrimination and retaliation in connection with his termination. The matter is currently in mediation through the EEOC's voluntary mediation program. The Company's employment counsel estimates a potential settlement range of $150,000 to $350,000.

X.B. State Tax — California Nexus Exposure
The Target currently files state income tax returns in Ohio, Michigan, Indiana, Illinois, and Texas.
Our review of the Company's sales records, employee roster, and payroll data reveals that the Target has employed a full-time sales representative based in San Jose, California since January 2022. This individual operates from a home office, calls on customers in the California market, and is carried on the Company's payroll with a California mailing address. Additionally, the Company generated approximately $3.8 million in California-sourced sales in FY2024, with comparable revenue amounts in FY2022 and FY2023.
Despite this physical presence in California — which clearly establishes income tax nexus under both physical presence standards and California's economic nexus thresholds — the Target has not filed California income or franchise tax returns for any period since at least 2022.
The SPA tax representation (Section 3.14) states that the Company has "filed or caused to be filed all Tax Returns required to be filed in all jurisdictions in which such filing is required by applicable law." The Company's failure to file California returns since at least 2022 represents a clear potential breach of this representation.
Risk Flag. Estimated exposure includes: (a) California corporation franchise/income tax on California-apportioned income for FY2022 through FY2024 — the precise liability will depend on the applicable apportionment factors (single-sales-factor apportionment under California law), but based on the Company's California-sourced revenue and estimated profit margins, the cumulative tax liability could range from $100,000 to $300,000 or more across the three open years; (b) California minimum franchise tax of $800 per year for three years ($2,400); (c) failure-to-file penalties of 25% of the tax due for each delinquent year; and (d) statutory interest. The total exposure is uncertain but could be material depending on the full extent of the Company's California-apportioned income across all open years.
We recommend: (1) engaging California tax counsel to estimate the total exposure, including tax, penalties, and interest for all open years; (2) requiring the Seller to file all delinquent California returns and pay any resulting tax, penalties, and interest prior to closing, or structuring a tax indemnity and escrow to cover the estimated exposure; and (3) correcting the SPA tax representation and Disclosure Schedule 3.14 to disclose the California filing deficiency.

Critical Finding. Section 14.2 of the Monterrey lease agreement contains a consent-to-assignment clause requiring the landlord's prior written approval for any "direct or indirect change of control" of the lessee entity. The acquisition of 100% of PrecisionFlow's outstanding common stock by Cascadia constitutes an indirect change of control of PrecisionFlow de México (the lessee), thereby triggering this consent requirement.
Risk Flag. The SPA's Required Consents schedule (Schedule 5.3) does not list the Inmobiliaria del Norte landlord consent as a required consent for the transaction. This is a significant omission. The Monterrey facility accounts for $38.2 million in revenue — 26.7% of total FY2024 revenue. If the landlord does not consent to the change of control, PrecisionFlow de México could be in default under the lease, potentially resulting in lease termination and the loss of the Monterrey manufacturing operations. The cost, complexity, and timeline associated with relocating manufacturing operations in Monterrey would be substantial and could cause significant disruption to the Company's supply chain and customer deliveries.
We recommend: (1) immediately adding the Inmobiliaria del Norte consent to Schedule 5.3; (2) beginning the process of obtaining landlord consent well in advance of the June 30, 2025 target closing date; (3) evaluating whether landlord consent should be structured as a closing condition in the SPA; and (4) assessing the cost and timeline for relocating Monterrey operations if consent is ultimately refused.

XIV. Conclusion
PrecisionFlow Systems, Inc. is an attractive acquisition target with strong revenue growth, solid margins, and a complementary product portfolio that will enhance Cascadia's Fluid Power division. The Company's manufacturing capabilities in Dayton and Monterrey, its established customer relationships, and its proprietary technology in hydraulic control systems represent meaningful strategic value for the Buyer.
However, the discrepancies identified during diligence between the Seller's draft SPA representations and disclosure schedules, on the one hand, and our diligence findings, on the other, are significant in number and, in several cases, in potential financial impact. The critical items — including the incomplete IP disclosures surrounding the AquaDyne dispute, the undisclosed Kessler change-of-control termination right, the expired NorthPoint contract, the undisclosed environmental condition, the California tax nexus exposure, and the disputed Beecham share ownership — each require attention before the SPA can be executed with confidence that the Buyer's interests are adequately protected.
We recommend that the deal team schedule a call with the client team — specifically Margaret Tsai (CEO, Cascadia Industrial Holdings), David Kowalski (CFO), and Rajan Mehta (General Counsel) — to discuss these findings and prioritize which items must be resolved prior to signing on March 14, 2025 versus those that can be addressed as closing conditions or through indemnification structures in the SPA.
A redline of the SPA incorporating our recommended changes to the representations, disclosure schedules, and closing conditions will be circulated under separate cover.
